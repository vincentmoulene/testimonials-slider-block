A charity has little technical budget, a lot of printed material and one constant problem: turning an intention into an action before the person gets home and forgets.

## Donations: twelve seconds instead of three days

On a poster, in a local newsletter, on a market stall: a code that opens the donation page, with the amount pre-filled if you suggest one.

Two very different ways to do it:

**A link to a donation page.** Simple and familiar, but every card payment carries a processing fee — 1.5 to 3% that will not fund your project.

**A SEPA transfer QR code (GiroCode).** The donor scans from their banking app, which opens with your IBAN, your name and the amount already filled in. They confirm. **No commission**, and the money lands directly in the charity's account. It is read natively by German, Austrian, Dutch and Belgian banking apps, and increasingly across the rest of Europe.

For a charity the second option is worth testing: on €10,000 of annual donations, the difference is not symbolic.

## Membership and renewals

Renewal is the moment charities lose members — not through disaffection but through forgetting. A code on the newsletter, on the poster in the hall, at the bottom of the reminder letter: the form opens, the subscription is paid, it is done.

## Volunteering

A code on your stand at a volunteering fair, opening the sign-up form. The interested passer-by does not leave with a flyer they will lose: they are already on your list.

## The internal uses people forget

- **The AGM**: a code on the notice pointing to the papers; another in the room for voting or the attendance sheet.
- **Equipment**: a code on each item you lend out (PA system, tables, gazebo) linking to the booking sheet and its condition report.
- **Archives**: a code on the physical binder pointing to the digitised version.

## Two lessons from the field

1. **Say what is behind it.** "Scan to donate £10" converts far better than "Scan us". The promise has to be visible without scanning.
2. **Always keep a paper alternative.** Some of your donors are seventy-five and will never scan. A QR code adds a channel; it replaces none.

## The takeaway

For a charity, a QR code costs nothing and removes the delay between the feeling and the gesture. That delay is exactly where donations are lost.
