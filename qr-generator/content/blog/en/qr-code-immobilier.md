A For Sale board gives a phone number. The passer-by writes it down, or more likely does not. With a QR code they see the photos, the price, the floor area and the energy rating before calling you — and when they do call, they already know what they want.

## The board: qualifying before the first call

The code must open **the listing for that property**, not the agency's website. Photos, price, area, surveys, floor plan. The prospect who calls afterwards has passed the first filter on their own.

Practical constraints of a board:

- People scan from the pavement, often from a stopped car: allow **15 cm wide minimum** for a scan at 1.5 m, more if the board is high up.
- The board stays outdoors for months: use UV-resistant printing, or the contrast fades and the code dies.
- Plan for "sold": the page should then say sold and offer similar properties, not return a 404.

## The agency window

The real moment is Sunday: the agency is closed and people look anyway. One code per listing in the window, or a single code to the properties in the area, captures an audience that would otherwise have done nothing.

## The rental application pack

A code on the listing opening the list of required documents and the application form. You receive complete files instead of twenty emails with missing attachments.

## Handovers and management

Unglamorous, very useful:

- A code in the meter cupboard or plant room opening the equipment record, the last service date and the contractor's number.
- A code inside a holiday let opening the welcome guide.
- A code on the building's noticeboard, pointing to the minutes of the last owners' meeting.

## Three mistakes that cost you leads

1. **Linking to the agency home page.** The prospect wanted *that* property; they will not go hunting through two hundred listings.
2. **Encoding a URL with an internal reference** like `?ref=A12345&session=…`. It changes with your next software migration, and the printed code dies with it.
3. **Skipping the tracking.** Add `?utm_source=board`: after a quarter you know whether the boards are worth what they cost.

## The takeaway

In property, a QR code does not sell anything: it removes the delay between interest and information. And in this trade, delay is exactly what loses leads.
