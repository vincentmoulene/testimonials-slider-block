We all know how a business card ends: a pocket, a washing machine, a bin. A QR code changes one thing, and it is the decisive one — your details land in the address book before the card is lost.

## vCard or link: the real trade-off

**A vCard** encodes the contact inside the pattern. The scan immediately offers "Add to contacts". It works offline, in the basement of a trade-show hall where the network is saturated — which is exactly where cards get exchanged.

**A link** to your page or LinkedIn is richer and editable, but it needs a network, a page load and one more tap. Plenty of people stop before the contact ever gets saved.

For a business card: **vCard**. For a conference badge or a closing slide, a link is defensible, because the goal there is usually discovery rather than being called back.

## Which fields to include

The useful minimum: first name, last name, company, job title, one number, a work email, a website. That is all.

Every extra field makes the pattern denser. A vCard with a full postal address, two numbers, a fax and a note produces a code that is harder to scan at small size — exactly the size of a business card. Restraint here is not taste, it is legibility.

And only include what you are happy to hand out: a vCard is plain text, readable by anyone who decodes the image.

## Placement

The **back**, bottom right, 2 cm minimum. Keep the front for your name and logo: a QR code on the front makes the card read as an advert rather than an introduction.

Leave at least 4 mm of white around the code. This is the most common layout mistake — a code pushed to the edge of the card, or touching a block of colour, does not scan reliably.

## Beyond the card

- **Email signature**: the reader adds you in one scan from their phone while reading on their computer.
- **Conference badge**: on the lanyard, at chest height — the natural scanning distance in a conversation.
- **Closing slide**: projected large, it scans from the back of the room.
- **Shop window or practice door**: so people can reach you when you are closed.

## The takeaway

A restrained vCard, on the back, tested on two phones before printing. It is not an innovation; it simply removes the step where the other person had to copy ten lines by hand — the step nobody ever took.
