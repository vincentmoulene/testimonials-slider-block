A shop is closed roughly 70% of the time. A QR code in the window is the only member of staff who works on Sundays and at 11pm.

## The six placements that work

**1. The window.** A code opening the catalogue, click & collect or an appointment form. Put it at eye level, between 1.40 m and 1.60 m, and away from reflections: test it at the real hours, in full sun and after dark.

**2. The shelf edge.** Straight to the product page: full specs, sizes in stock, reviews. That is what the customer is already doing on their phone — except they are doing it on a competitor's site.

**3. The product itself.** Instructions, a video tutorial, the warranty, care advice. You remove the paper leaflet nobody reads and everybody loses.

**4. The receipt.** Reviews, loyalty programme, returns. The customer has just bought: it is the only moment they are genuinely available.

**5. The empty shelf.** A "tell me when it's back" or "order it now" code turns a stockout into a delayed sale instead of a lost one.

**6. The bag.** The customer walks home holding your code.

## The mistake that cancels everything: linking to the home page

A code on a shoe label that opens `myshop.com` has failed. The customer wanted *that* shoe and lands on a home page, a menu, a cookie banner — and gives up.

Every code must open **the exact page**, with no intermediate step. If your site cannot give each product its own URL, fix that before printing anything.

## Measuring without adding tools

Add a parameter to the URL: `?utm_source=window`, `?utm_source=shelf-shoes`. Your existing web analytics then separates each placement, with no extra tool and no subscription. After a month you know which ones are worth reprinting.

## Three practical details

- **Size.** On a label, 2 cm minimum, and export the SVG: rescaling a PNG is what makes small codes unreadable.
- **The words next to it.** "See the sizes in stock" converts far better than "Scan me". Say what is behind it.
- **Lifespan.** A shelf label stays out for months. Encode a URL you control and that will not move — you can always change the redirect later.

## The takeaway

A QR code in a shop does not replace a salesperson: it answers the questions customers do not dare ask, and it captures the visits that happen outside opening hours. Start with the window and the empty shelf — those two pay off immediately.
